Church Clerk — Landing Page Design System

1. Purpose

Church Clerk is a professional church management platform designed to help churches manage their people, branches, communication, attendance, giving, reports, and day-to-day operations from one system.

The landing page must communicate:

- Trust
- Simplicity
- Organization
- Professionalism
- Human connection
- Church-specific understanding
- Modern technology without looking like an AI product

The page should feel like a real product with a strong brand, not a generic SaaS template.

---

2. Core Design Direction

Brand personality

Church Clerk should feel:

- Premium
- Calm
- Trustworthy
- Modern
- Warm
- Human
- Organized
- Confident
- Purpose-built
- Mature

It should NOT feel:

- Futuristic
- Cyberpunk
- Overly technical
- Corporate and cold
- Like an AI startup
- Like a template
- Over-designed

Core principle

«Design Church Clerk like a serious product company that understands churches deeply — not like an AI-generated SaaS website.»

---

3. STRICTLY AVOID

Do NOT automatically fall back to common AI/SaaS landing-page patterns.

Avoid:

- Purple/blue AI gradients everywhere
- Neon glows
- Excessive glassmorphism
- Floating translucent cards everywhere
- Huge gradient text
- Excessive pill-shaped UI
- Repetitive rounded cards
- Generic three-column feature grids
- Every section using identical card layouts
- Generic dashboard mockups floating beside a hero
- Excessive blobs and abstract shapes
- Stock-photo-heavy layouts
- Excessive shadows
- Excessive animations
- Overly rounded interfaces
- "Revolutionize your church"
- "Empower your ministry"
- "Transform your church forever"
- Empty marketing buzzwords
- Fake statistics
- Fake testimonials
- Fake customer logos
- Invented product capabilities

Do not introduce a visual pattern simply because it is common in AI-generated landing pages.

---

4. Visual Philosophy

Church Clerk should use editorial product design.

Think:

- Strong typography
- Large intentional whitespace
- Interesting composition
- Product UI integrated into the story
- Asymmetric layouts when appropriate
- Carefully controlled borders
- Subtle depth
- Real interface screenshots
- Strong section hierarchy
- Visual rhythm

The page should not feel like:

Hero → Cards → Cards → Cards → Testimonials → CTA

Instead, every section should have a different visual composition.

---

5. Typography

Typography should communicate confidence and clarity.

Headings

Use a strong modern sans-serif.

Headings should be:

- Large
- Clear
- Relatively tight
- Confident
- Short

Avoid extremely decorative fonts.

Body

Body text should be:

- Highly readable
- Comfortable line height
- Moderate width
- Never overly dense

Hierarchy

Use a clear hierarchy:

- Eyebrow
- Large headline
- Supporting paragraph
- Primary action
- Secondary action

Don't make every piece of text visually loud.

---

6. Color System

Use Church Clerk's existing brand colors if they already exist in the application.

Do NOT invent a completely different landing-page palette.

The landing page must visually belong to the same product as the dashboard.

General color philosophy

Use:

- One primary brand color
- Supporting neutral colors
- Strong dark text
- Soft background variations
- Subtle borders
- Carefully controlled accent colors

Avoid:

- Rainbow palettes
- Multiple competing accent colors
- Neon colors
- Heavy gradients

Gradients may be used only when they serve a deliberate visual purpose.

---

7. Spacing

Use generous whitespace.

The landing page should breathe.

Prefer:

- Large section padding
- Clear separation between ideas
- Comfortable text widths
- Consistent vertical rhythm

Do not fill empty space simply because it exists.

Whitespace is part of the design.

---

8. Border Radius

Use moderate corner rounding.
Avoid making everything extremely rounded.

Cards, buttons, screenshots and panels should feel like part of a professional product interface rather than a collection of floating bubbles.

---

9. Shadows

Use shadows sparingly.

Prefer:

- Borders
- Contrast
- Layering
- Background differences

over heavy drop shadows.

When shadows are used, they should communicate depth rather than decoration.

---

10. Motion

Animations should be subtle and purposeful.

Good:

- Fade-in
- Small translate transitions
- Product UI movement
- Hover states
- Scroll-based reveals
- Chart animation
- Image transitions

Avoid:

- Constant floating
- Excessive bouncing
- Large parallax effects
- Distracting text animations
- Animation on every element

Motion should support the story.

---

11. Landing Page Story

The page should answer these questions in order:

1. What is Church Clerk?

Immediately explain that it is a church management platform.

2. Why does it matter?

Show the operational problems churches face:

- Member information scattered across systems
- Difficult branch management
- Manual communication
- Attendance tracking
- Giving records
- Reporting
- Administrative workload

3. What does Church Clerk actually do?

Show the product.

4. How does it help different church operations?

Introduce:

- Members
- Branches
- Attendance
- Communication
- Giving
- Reports

5. Why should a church trust it?

Communicate:

- Security
- Organization
- Multi-branch support
- Permissions
- Reliable reporting
- Professional infrastructure

6. What should the visitor do?

Clear CTA:

- Start Free
- Get Started
- Request a Demo

Do not overwhelm users with too many CTAs.

---

12. Recommended Page Structure

HERO

The hero should NOT simply be:

"Manage your church better."

Instead, communicate a specific value proposition.

Suggested structure:

Eyebrow:

"Church Management, Simplified."

Headline:

"Everything your church needs to stay organized."

Supporting text:

"Manage members, branches, communication, attendance, giving and reports from one place."

Actions:

Primary:
"Get Started"

Secondary:
"See How It Works"

Visual:

Use an authentic Church Clerk product interface.

Do NOT create a generic floating SaaS dashboard.

The product should feel tangible.

---

13. Problem / Reality Section

Introduce the reality churches face.

Possible direction:

"Church administration shouldn't feel scattered."

Use a strong editorial composition.

Show several operational problems visually:

Member records
Branch information
Attendance
Communication
Reports

Avoid six identical cards.

Use different visual treatments where appropriate.

---

14. Product Introduction

Introduce Church Clerk as the central system.

Example concept:

"One place for the work behind the ministry."

Show the actual product interface.

Use large product imagery.

Allow the UI to become part of the visual identity.

---

Do NOT create a generic feature grid.

Instead, give important features their own visual moments.

Members

Show:

- Member records
- Profiles
- Status
- Departments
- Membership information

Branches

Show:

- Headquarters
- Branch overview
- Individual branches
- Branch activity
- Consolidated information

Communication

Show:

- Announcements

Attendance

Show:

- Attendance records
- Trends

- Reports

Giving

Show:

- Offerings
- Tithes
- Special funds
- Giving trends
- Reports

Reports

Show:

- Report generation

- PDF

---

16. Product Screenshots

Whenever possible, use actual Church Clerk UI.

Never fabricate product capabilities.

Screenshots should:

- Be crisp
- Show realistic data
- Have intentional cropping
- Be integrated into the layout
- Support the message of the section

Do not simply place a screenshot inside a generic rounded rectangle.

Consider: - Large cropped interface
- Full-width interface
- Side-by-side interface
- Interactive-looking interface
- Editorial product close-up

---

17. Multi-Branch Story

Church Clerk should clearly communicate that it works for churches with multiple branches.

Possible visual narrative:

Headquarters
↓
Branches
↓
Members
↓
Activity
↓
Reports

The design should communicate centralized visibility without making the interface feel complicated.

---

18. Trust / Security

Keep this section mature and understated.

Discuss capabilities that actually exist, such as:

- Role-based permissions
- Branch-level access
- Secure authentication
- Multi-tenant architecture
- Administrative controls

Do not make exaggerated security claims.

Do not use fake security badges.

---

19. Pricing / Conversion

Pricing should be clear and simple.

Avoid:

- Artificial urgency
- Fake discounts
- Countdown timers
- Manipulative copy

Clearly communicate:

- Free/trial availability
- Plans
- Included features
- SMS/usage considerations where relevant

The user should understand what they're getting.

---

20. Final CTA

The final section should feel like the natural conclusion of the story.

Possible direction:

"Your church deserves better tools for the work behind the scenes."

Then:

"Start using Church Clerk"

Primary CTA:

"Get Started"

Keep the final CTA visually strong but not aggressive.

---

21. Responsive Design

The design must work naturally across:

- Desktop
- Laptop
- Tablet
- Mobile

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout.

Mobile layouts should be intentionally redesigned.

Pay special attention to:

- Hero typography
- Screenshot cropping
- Navigation
- CTA placement
- Section spacing
- Horizontal overflow
- Tables/product UI

---

22. Accessibility

Maintain:

- Strong color contrast
- Keyboard accessibility
- Visible focus states
- Semantic HTML
- Proper heading hierarchy
- Accessible buttons
- Meaningful alt text
- Reduced-motion support

Accessibility should not be sacrificed for visual effects.

---

23. Copywriting Rules

Church Clerk copy should be:

- Clear
- Human
- Specific
- Confident
- Concise

Avoid marketing clichés.

Do not repeatedly use:

"empower"

"revolutionize"

"transform"

"unlock"

"seamlessly"

"next-generation"

"game-changing"

"AI-powered"

unless genuinely relevant.

Prefer language that explains what the product actually does.

---

24. Design Consistency With Dashboard

The landing page and application must feel like the same product.

Reuse:

- Brand colors
- Typography
- Button language
- Border treatment
- Icon style
- Data visualization style
- Spacing principles

The landing page may be more expressive than the dashboard, but it must not look like a different company.

---

25. Component Rules

Create reusable landing-page components where appropriate.

Examples:

- Hero
- SectionHeader
- ProductShowcase
- FeatureStory
- Testimonial
- Pricing
- CTA
- LogoCloud
- ScreenshotFrame

But don't force every section into the same component structure.

Reusable code does not mean identical visual layouts.

---

26. Engineering Rules

Before modifying the landing page:

1. Inspect the existing application.
2. Inspect the existing design system.
3. Inspect existing brand colors.
4. Inspect existing typography.
5. Inspect reusable components.
6. Inspect routing.
7. Inspect responsive behavior.
8. Inspect existing landing-page code.

Do not unnecessarily rewrite unrelated application code.

Do not modify dashboard functionality while working on the landing page.

Do not introduce a new UI library without justification.

Do not replace existing working components simply because another implementation looks different.

---

27. Design Process

When asked to create or redesign the landing page:

Step 1 — Analyze

First inspect the existing design and identify:

- What works
- What doesn't
- Existing design tokens
- Existing components
- Existing product UI

Step 2 — Plan

Create a section-by-section visual plan.

For every section define:
- Purpose
- Message
- Visual composition
- Product UI required
- CTA
- Responsive behavior

Step 3 — Implement

Implement one major section at a time.

Step 4 — Review

After implementation check:

- Does it look generic?
- Is the visual rhythm varied?
- Are sections too repetitive?
- Does the product feel authentic?
- Does it look like Church Clerk?
- Does it match the dashboard?
- Is there unnecessary decoration?

Step 5 — Refine

Fix visual problems before adding more sections.

---

28. IMPORTANT AGENT BEHAVIOR

When the user gives a visual instruction, do not immediately translate it into generic SaaS components.

Interpret the instruction as a design problem.

If the user says:

"Make this premium"

consider:

- Typography
- Spacing
- Composition
- Contrast
- Product imagery
- Hierarchy
- Motion
- Density

Do not simply add:

- gradients
- shadows
- rounded cards
- animations

If the user says:

"Make it modern"

do not automatically use:

- glassmorphism
- neon gradients
- floating cards

Modern design can also mean:

- excellent typography
- strong whitespace
- restrained color
- intentional composition
- precise interaction design

---

29. DO NOT INVENT

Never invent:

- Customers
- Testimonials
- Churches
- Statistics
- Revenue figures
- Member counts
- Security certifications
- Integrations
- Product features

Use placeholders only when explicitly requested.

---

30. Final Quality Standard

Before considering the landing page complete, ask:

«If the Church Clerk logo were removed, would this still look like a generic AI-generated SaaS website?»

If yes, redesign it.

Ask:

«Does every section have a reason to exist?»

Ask:

«Does the page tell a coherent story?»

Ask:

«Does the product itself remain the visual hero?»

Ask:

«Would a professional product designer be comfortable presenting this to a real church organization?»

If the answer is no, continue refining.

---

Windsurf Implementation Rule

The landing page is a brand experience, not merely another React page.

Do not optimize only for:

- component reuse
- speed of implementation
- number of sections
- number of animations
- visual effects

Optimize for:

Clarity + Trust + Product authenticity + Visual distinction + Conversion.

When uncertain between a generic SaaS pattern and a more intentional composition, prefer the intentional composition.

Do not make design decisions solely because they are common in AI-generated websites.

Always preserve existing Church Clerk functionality while improving the visual experience.
